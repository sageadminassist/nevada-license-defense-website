Examples of Actions Under NRS 630.301

The following are examples of physician conduct that could result in disciplinary action by the Nevada Board of Medical Examiners:

  • ◆Disciplinary action taken by another jurisdiction
  • ◆Surrender of a previous license while under investigation
  • ◆Malpractice or gross malpractice
  • ◆Engaging in sexual activity with a patient
  • ◆Disruptive behavior
  • ◆Violating or exploiting the trust of a patient for financial or personal gain
  • ◆Failure to offer appropriate care with intent to influence financial well-being
  • ◆Engaging in disreputable conduct
  • ◆Sexual contact with surrogate of patient or relatives of patient
  • ◆Obtaining license by bribery, fraud, or misrepresentation
  • ◆False advertising or practicing under another name
  • ◆Signing blank prescription forms
  • ◆Discouraging a patient from seeking a second opinion
  • ◆Terminating care without adequate notice
  • ◆Accepting compensation to influence evaluation or treatment
  • ◆Inappropriate division of fees or referral arrangements
  • ◆Charging for services not rendered
  • ◆Aiding the practice of an unlicensed person
  • ◆Failing to disclose a conflict of interest
  • ◆Improper prescribing or administering of controlled substances
  • ◆Inability to practice medicine with reasonable skill and safety
  • ◆Violation of any law relating to controlled substances
  • ◆Filing of false reports or failure to report required information
  • ◆Failure to maintain proper medical records
  • ◆Altering or destroying medical records
  • ◆Failure to supervise a medical assistant
  • ◆Unauthorized injection of dermal fillers or botulinum toxin

Nevada Medical Board Defense FAQ

What law governs the Nevada Board of Medical Examiners?

The Board of Medical Examiners licenses and disciplines MDs, physician assistants, and respiratory care practitioners under NRS Chapter 630. Grounds for discipline are listed in NRS 630.301 through 630.3065.

What happens after a complaint is filed with the Board of Medical Examiners?

A board investigator reviews the complaint and may request records or a written response. An investigative committee then decides whether to dismiss it, issue a letter of warning, or authorize a formal complaint that proceeds to a hearing under NRS Chapter 233B.

Can a malpractice lawsuit lead to a medical board investigation?

Yes. Malpractice settlements and judgments can trigger board review. Your malpractice carrier's attorney represents the civil case — your license often needs separate counsel.

Should I talk to the Nevada State Board of Medical Examiners before I hire a lawyer?

No. Anything you say or write to the board — including an informal phone call with an investigator — can be used in the case against you. Speak with a Nevada license defense attorney first. Craig K. Perry offers a free 30–45 minute consultation at 702.893.4777.
