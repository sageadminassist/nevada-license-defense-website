For 100 years, the Nevada State Board of Nursing (NSBN) has regulated nursing to ensure safe practices and public safety. The NSBN regulates more than 70,000 nurses statewide and has broad authority to investigate, discipline, and revoke licenses.

When your nursing license is threatened, you need experienced legal representation. Craig K. Perry has defended nurses before the NSBN for over 16 years — from initial investigations through formal hearings and reinstatement proceedings.

Nevada nursing licenses are governed by the Nevada Nurse Practice Act (NRS Chapter 632) and administrative regulations under NAC Chapter 632. The NSBN operates under NRS 233B (Nevada Administrative Procedure Act) for all disciplinary hearings and appeals.

Anything you say to the Board can and will be used against you if you self-incriminate. Sometimes things you say orally are misheard or misunderstood. Consult with an experienced attorney first.

Common Reasons Nurses Are Investigated

Fraudulent Application

False information or concealment in an application for licensure, renewal, or endorsement — sometimes unintentional, such as not reporting a resolved criminal conviction.

Failure to Meet Standards of Care

Neglecting necessary care, providing substandard care, or failing to properly monitor and assess a patient's condition.

Medication Diversion

Diverting controlled substances for personal use, failing to document wastage, or signing as witness to wastage not actually witnessed.

Falsifying Documents

Creating, altering, or destroying records — including medication administration records, nursing assessments, timecards, or CE certificates.

Lack of Collaboration

Not reporting changes in a patient's condition, not following physician orders, or not communicating effectively with other healthcare team members.

Failing CE Audits

Failure to retain proof of required CE courses. There is NO TIME LIMIT for keeping the bioterrorism class record — you must retain proof until audited, which could be decades later.

Expired License

Practicing on an expired license, or failure to renew before the expiration date.

Intent to Deceive

Lying to patients or colleagues, misrepresenting telehealth or home-health visits, or using prior vital signs instead of taking new ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I contact an attorney?

As soon as possible. You always have more options the earlier you start — and the initial consultation is free. If you've received a Notice of Investigation, the NSBN typically expects a response within 14 days.

Do I have to report an arrest to the Board?

Not always — arrests don't automatically need to be reported, but convictions must be handled carefully. Talk with an attorney before disclosing anything.

My employer told me to self-report. Should I?

Self-reporting deserves careful thought — it can carry real consequences. Discuss your situation with experienced counsel before you act.

Someone from the Board called me — should I talk to them?

Be careful. Anything you say can be used against you. Consult an attorney before responding or calling back.

Can I get a revoked license back?

Often yes. After a period of time, nurses whose licenses were revoked or surrendered may pursue reinstatement. We can help you build a plan to return to practice.
