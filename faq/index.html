Getting Help & Working With Craig

When should I contact an attorney about a board complaint?+

As soon as possible — ideally before you respond to the board at all. You always have more options the earlier you start, and the initial consultation with Craig K. Perry is free. If you've received a Notice of Investigation, the board typically expects a response within 14 days.

Do I have to self-report to my licensing board?+

It depends on your license type, the nature of the issue, and the specific circumstances. Self-reporting can carry real consequences and should not be done without first consulting an attorney. We can help you determine your obligations and how to respond strategically.

What happens at a disciplinary hearing?+

A disciplinary hearing is an administrative proceeding before your licensing board. You have the right to be represented by an attorney, to present evidence, and to cross-examine witnesses. The outcome can range from a reprimand or fine to license suspension or revocation. Having experienced legal representation significantly improves your odds.

Can I keep practicing while under investigation?+

Usually yes — a license investigation does not automatically suspend your license. However, the board can seek an emergency suspension in serious cases. Contact us immediately if you receive any communication from your licensing board.

How long does a board investigation take?+

It varies widely — from a few months to over a year, depending on the complexity of the case and the board involved. Some matters can be resolved quickly through informal conferences; others proceed to formal hearings. We'll give you a realistic picture of your timeline at the outset.

What does it cost to hire Craig K. Perry?+

Fees vary by case complexity. The initial consultation is always free. We'll discuss fee structures at that consultation — including whether a flat fee, retainer, or other arrangement makes sense for your situation.

My malpractice insurer assigned me an attorney — do I still need my own?+

It depends. Malpractice defense attorneys represent your insurer's interests, which may not align perfectly with yours — particularly when it comes to your license. A separate attorney focused on your license defense can be critical, especially if the board investigation parallels a civil case.

Can a revoked or surrendered license be reinstated?+

Often yes. After a specified period (which varies by board and by the nature of the original action), you may petition for reinstatement. We can help you build the strongest possible case for returning to practice.

The Nevada Board Discipline Process

Can a Nevada licensing board suspend my license before a hearing?+

Yes, in limited cases. Under NRS 233B.127, a board can order an emergency (summary) suspension when it finds that public health, safety, or welfare requires immediate action. A prompt hearing must follow. Most investigations do not involve a summary suspension.

More on Board Discipline Process →
Is a Notice of Investigation the same as discipline?+

No. An investigation is not discipline, and many investigations end in dismissal or a non-disciplinary letter. How you respond at this stage strongly affects whether the case goes further.

More on Board Discipline Process →
Do I have to tell my employer about a board investigation?+

It depends on your license, your employment contract, and facility policies. Some obligations are mandatory; others are not. Ask your attorney before disclosing.

More on Board Discipline Process →
Will board discipline show up publicly?+

Formal discipline is generally public and appears on the board's license lookup. Dismissals and many non-disciplinary outcomes are not. Health-care discipline is also typically reported to the National Practitioner Data Bank.

More on Board Discipline Process →
How long does the Nevada board discipline process take?+

Anywhere from a few months to more than a year, depending on the board, the complexity of the allegations, expert review, and whether the case settles or goes to hearing.

Drugs & Substances

Will I be criminally charged for drug diversion in Nevada?+

Not necessarily. Many diversion cases are handled only by the licensing board. But boards and employers can refer cases to law enforcement, so anything you say should be treated as if it could be used in a criminal case.

More on Drug Diversion →
Can I keep my license after a diversion allegation?+

Often, yes. Outcomes range from dismissal to monitoring agreements, probation, suspension, or revocation. Early representation and a well-documented response make a significant difference.

More on Drug Diversion →
Should I agree to a substance-use evaluation?+

Talk to your attorney first. Who performs the evaluation, what records they see, and how the results are used all matter.

More on Drug Diversion →
Can the Nevada board investigate me based only on PMP data?+

Yes. PMP data can prompt an investigation on its own, but data alone doesn't prove improper prescribing — documentation and expert review do.

More on Prescribing & Controlled Substances →
Does the Board of Pharmacy have authority over physicians?+

It can regulate practitioners who dispense or hold controlled-substance registrations in Nevada, in addition to the practitioner's own board.

More on Prescribing & Controlled Substances →
What happens after an expert reviews my charts?+

If the expert finds no violation, cases often close. If the expert criticizes care, you can respond with your own expert and argument before the board decides whether to file charges.

More on Prescribing & Controlled Substances →
Will a positive drug test end my career?+

Usually not. Many professionals complete evaluation and monitoring and continue practicing. How the case is handled early matters a great deal.

More on Impairment & Substance Use →
Is a monitoring program public discipline?+

It depends on the board and how the agreement is structured. Some arrangements can be non-public; others are formal discipline.

More on Impairment & Substance Use →
What if my prescribed medication caused the positive result?+

Provide documentation through your attorney. Medical review of the result can show a legitimate prescription.

More on Impairment & Substance Use →
What happens if I miss a random drug test on probation?+

It is usually treated as a violation and can be treated like a positive test. Outcomes range from a warning to suspension, depending on your history and explanation.

More on Probation Violations →
What is a dilute test and why does it matter?+

A dilute specimen has low concentration and may be treated as suspicious. Medical explanations, such as hydration or certain conditions, can matter.

More on Probation Violations →
Can my probation be extended instead of my license suspended?+

Often yes. Renegotiated terms, including longer probation, are a common resolution for many violations.

More on Probation Violations →
Can I lose my Nevada nursing license for legal marijuana use?+

Legal off-duty use alone is not usually grounds for discipline, but a positive test can lead to employer action and a board inquiry. Use while on probation or monitoring can be a violation.

More on Marijuana Use →
Can trace amounts of THC cost me my job?+

Yes. Employment may still be jeopardized even without evidence of impairment, depending on the employer's policy.

More on Marijuana Use →
What about CBD?+

Some CBD products contain THC and can cause a positive test. Keep product documentation and get advice.

Patient Care & Practice

Does a malpractice settlement mean the board will discipline me?+

No. Settlements can trigger review, but boards make their own judgment about the care, and many reviews close without discipline.

More on Negligence & Patient Harm →
Do I need a separate lawyer if my insurer gave me one?+

Often, yes. The insurer's lawyer is focused on the civil claim. A license defense lawyer focuses on keeping your license and record clean.

More on Negligence & Patient Harm →
Who decides if I met the standard of care?+

Usually an expert reviewer in your field advises the board. You can submit your own expert opinion in response.

More on Negligence & Patient Harm →
Can an RN perform Botox or filler injections in Nevada?+

Only within the limits of Nevada law, the nurse's license, and proper physician or APRN orders and supervision. Med spa arrangements frequently fall short — get advice before taking on aesthetic work.

More on Practicing Outside Scope →
My employer told me to do it. Am I still responsible?+

You can be. Boards hold each licensee responsible for staying within their own scope, but the employer's direction is important context in your defense.

More on Practicing Outside Scope →
Is a scope complaint always public discipline?+

No. Many are resolved with education or a non-disciplinary outcome, especially when the licensee responds well early.

More on Practicing Outside Scope →
Can an abuse allegation lead to emergency suspension?+

Yes, if the board believes there is an immediate risk to patients. Most cases do not involve emergency suspension, but early representation is critical.

What if the allegation comes from a confused patient?+

The board must still investigate, but reliability of the account, corroboration, and records matter a great deal to the outcome.

More on Patient Abuse & Neglect →
Is neglect different from abuse?+

Yes. Neglect usually involves failure to provide required care, often tied to staffing and workload, while abuse involves intentional harmful conduct.

Records, Billing & Privacy

Is a charting mistake grounds for discipline in Nevada?+

It can be, but boards distinguish isolated errors from patterns and from intentional falsification. Many honest errors resolve without public discipline.

More on Charting & Documentation →
Can I add a late entry to fix a chart?+

Only following your facility's late-entry policy, clearly labeled and dated, and never after you learn of a complaint without legal advice.

More on Charting & Documentation →
Why does 'dishonesty' matter so much?+

Boards view dishonesty as a character issue that goes to fitness to practice, so it can lead to harsher discipline than the clinical issue itself.

More on Charting & Documentation →
Can a HIPAA violation cost me my Nevada license?+

It can lead to discipline for unprofessional conduct, though most privacy cases result in lesser outcomes such as reprimands or education, especially for first-time, non-malicious conduct.

More on HIPAA & Privacy Breaches →
I looked up my own chart. Is that a violation?+

Many employers prohibit it unless done through the patient portal. It's usually treated less seriously than accessing others' records, but it can still lead to discipline.

More on HIPAA & Privacy Breaches →
Does the Nevada board enforce HIPAA?+

No — federal regulators enforce HIPAA. But the board can discipline the same conduct as unprofessional conduct under your licensing statute.

More on HIPAA & Privacy Breaches →
Can billing errors lead to license discipline in Nevada?+

Yes, especially if the board finds dishonesty. Honest errors with corrective action usually fare much better.

More on Billing & Insurance Fraud →
Should I wait until the criminal case is over?+

No. License strategy should be coordinated from the start, because statements in one case can affect the others.

More on Billing & Insurance Fraud →
Does repayment end the board case?+

Not automatically, but repayment and corrective action can significantly improve the license outcome.

It depends on your board and whether it is an arrest, charge, or conviction. Some reporting is triggered only by conviction or on renewal. Get advice for your specific license before reporting.

A single DUI rarely leads to revocation on its own, but it can lead to an investigation, evaluation, or monitoring, especially if there are signs of substance-use disorder or it is not disclosed.

Investigations are generally confidential. Formal discipline is typically public, which is why resolving a case before formal charges matters.

What law governs the Nevada State Board of Pharmacy?+

The Board of Pharmacy operates under NRS Chapter 639 and its regulations in NAC Chapter 639. Disciplinary hearings follow the Nevada Administrative Procedure Act, NRS Chapter 233B.

More on Nevada State Board of Pharmacy →
Does the Board of Pharmacy affect doctors and APRNs?+

Yes. Physicians, physician assistants, and APRNs who dispense medications or hold a Nevada controlled substance registration can be investigated by the Board of Pharmacy, separately from their own licensing board.

More on Nevada State Board of Pharmacy →
What are common Board of Pharmacy complaints?+

Dispensing errors, controlled-substance inventory discrepancies, diversion allegations, record-keeping violations, prescription monitoring program (PMP) issues, and impairment or substance use concerns.

More on Nevada State Board of Pharmacy →
What law governs the Nevada Board of Medical Examiners?+

The Board of Medical Examiners licenses and disciplines MDs, physician assistants, and respiratory care practitioners under NRS Chapter 630. Grounds for discipline are listed in NRS 630.301 through 630.3065.

More on Nevada State Board of Medical Examiners →
What happens after a complaint is filed with the Board of Medical Examiners?+

A board investigator reviews the complaint and may request records or a written response. An investigative committee then decides whether to dismiss it, issue a letter of warning, or authorize a formal complaint that proceeds to a hearing under NRS Chapter 233B.

More on Nevada State Board of Medical Examiners →
Can a malpractice lawsuit lead to a medical board investigation?+

Yes. Malpractice settlements and judgments can trigger board review. Your malpractice carrier's attorney represents the civil case — your license often needs separate counsel.

More on Nevada State Board of Medical Examiners →
What law governs the Nevada State Board of Osteopathic Medicine?+

Osteopathic physicians (DOs) in Nevada are licensed and disciplined under NRS Chapter 633. Contested cases are heard under the Nevada Administrative Procedure Act, NRS Chapter 233B.

More on Nevada State Board of Osteopathic Medicine →
Is a DO disciplined by the same board as an MD in Nevada?+

No. MDs are regulated by the Board of Medical Examiners (NRS 630); DOs are regulated by the separate State Board of Osteopathic Medicine (NRS 633).
