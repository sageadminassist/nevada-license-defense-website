Quick answer

Boundary complaints — dual relationships, inappropriate communication, social-media contact, or sexual misconduct allegations — are investigated aggressively by Nevada boards and can lead to summary suspension. They often come down to one person's word against another's, which makes early, careful handling of statements, texts, and witnesses essential. These matters are confidential at the investigation stage; keep them that way by speaking only with counsel.

By Craig K. Perry, Attorney at Law · Last updated November 2026 · General information, not legal advice for your specific case.

How This Allegation Shows Up

  • Dual or personal relationships with clients or patients
  • Texting, social media, or gifts outside professional channels
  • Allegations of inappropriate touching or comments
  • Custody or family-court-related complaints against therapists
  • Workplace harassment or disruptive-conduct reports

How Nevada Boards Handle It

Boards may interview the complainant, coworkers, and you, and review messages and records. Serious allegations can prompt immediate restrictions, such as chaperone requirements, or emergency suspension. Many behavioral-health complaints arise from contested custody matters and require careful context.

See the full Nevada board discipline process, step by step.

How We Defend These Cases

  • Preserve and review all communications early
  • Identify credibility problems and motives behind the complaint
  • Prepare you thoroughly before any board interview
  • Coordinate with criminal counsel if police are involved

What to Do Right Now

  1. Do not contact the complainant for any reason
  2. Do not delete messages or social media
  3. Do not give an interview to the board or employer without counsel

Boundary & Misconduct FAQ

Are boundary complaints public in Nevada?

Investigations are generally confidential. Formal discipline is typically public, which is why resolving a case before formal charges matters.

Can a former client's custody dispute lead to a board complaint?

Yes. Therapists and evaluators frequently receive complaints from parties in custody cases. These require a careful response that respects confidentiality rules.

Can my license be suspended immediately?

In serious cases, a board can order emergency suspension under NRS 233B.127 pending a prompt hearing.
