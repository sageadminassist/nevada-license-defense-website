Practicing Outside Your Scope: Nevada Board Defense

Call 702.893.4777Free Consultation

Quick answer

Scope-of-practice complaints allege that a licensee performed work beyond what their license, training, or supervision allows — for example, an RN acting on standing orders that do not exist, an LPN performing RN assessments, an APRN or PA practicing outside their authorization, or anyone administering injectables at a med spa without proper licensure and supervision. Nevada boards take these seriously because they bear directly on patient safety, but many cases involve unclear employer policies or instructions from supervisors. The key is showing what you were trained, authorized, and told to do.

By Craig K. Perry, Attorney at Law · Last updated November 2026 · General information, not legal advice for your specific case.

How This Allegation Shows Up

  • Dermal fillers, Botox, IV hydration, or laser work at med spas
  • LPNs or CNAs performing tasks reserved for RNs
  • APRNs or PAs prescribing or treating beyond their authorization
  • Acting without a valid order or outside facility protocols
  • Improper delegation to unlicensed staff
  • Practicing in Nevada on an out-of-state or expired license

How Nevada Boards Handle It

Boards examine your license level, written job description, facility policies, physician orders, supervision arrangements, and training records. Unauthorized cosmetic injections are a recurring focus of the Board of Medical Examiners. Some cases are referred to other boards when a supervising physician or employer is also involved.

See the full Nevada board discipline process, step by step.

How We Defend These Cases

  • Gather job descriptions, protocols, orders, and training records
  • Show the employer's or supervisor's role in the assignment
  • Separate a genuine scope issue from a paperwork gap
  • Negotiate education rather than discipline where appropriate

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop performing the task in question until your scope is clarified
  2. Save copies of protocols, orders, and messages from supervisors
  3. Talk to a license defense attorney before giving a statement

Practicing Outside Scope FAQ

Can an RN perform Botox or filler injections in Nevada?

Only within the limits of Nevada law, the nurse's license, and proper physician or APRN orders and supervision. Med spa arrangements frequently fall short — get advice before taking on aesthetic work.

My employer told me to do it. Am I still responsible?

You can be. Boards hold each licensee responsible for staying within their own scope, but the employer's direction is important context in your defense.

Is a scope complaint always public discipline?

No. Many are resolved with education or a non-disciplinary outcome, especially when the licensee responds well early.
