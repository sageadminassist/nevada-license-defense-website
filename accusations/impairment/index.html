Quick answer

Nevada licensing boards treat impairment — from alcohol, drugs, or a health condition — as a safety issue, but most also offer a path to keep practicing through evaluation, treatment, and monitoring. Positive or missed drug screens, reports of impairment at work, and DUI arrests commonly start these cases. Choosing the right evaluator, understanding what a monitoring agreement really requires, and avoiding unnecessary admissions can mean the difference between confidential monitoring and public discipline.

By Craig K. Perry, Attorney at Law · Last updated November 2026 · General information, not legal advice for your specific case.

How This Allegation Shows Up

  • Positive pre-employment, random, or for-cause drug screen
  • Reports of impairment or odor of alcohol at work
  • DUI or drug-related arrest
  • Missed check-ins or positive tests while in a monitoring program
  • Mental-health conditions affecting practice

How Nevada Boards Handle It

Boards typically require an evaluation by an approved provider, then may offer treatment and a multi-year monitoring agreement with testing, meetings, and workplace restrictions. Non-compliance is a common reason licenses are later suspended, so the agreement's terms matter.

What to Do Right Now

  1. Don't refuse or skip a test without advice — refusal is often treated as positive
  2. Don't sign a monitoring or treatment contract before a lawyer reads it
  3. Get prescriptions documented for any medication that could show on a screen

Impairment & Substance Use FAQ

Will a positive drug test end my career?

Usually not. Many professionals complete evaluation and monitoring and continue practicing. How the case is handled early matters a great deal.

Is a monitoring program public discipline?

It depends on the board and how the agreement is structured. Some arrangements can be non-public; others are formal discipline.

What if my prescribed medication caused the positive result?

Provide documentation through your attorney. Medical review of the result can show a legitimate prescription.
