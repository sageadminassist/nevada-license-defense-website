Quick answer

A DUI or other arrest does not automatically cost a Nevada professional their license, but how the criminal case ends — and what you disclose, when — can trigger a board investigation. Reporting duties differ by board and by whether it is an arrest, a charge, or a conviction, and renewal applications ask specific questions. A plea that looks minor in criminal court can carry license consequences, so coordinate the criminal case with license counsel before you plead.

By Craig K. Perry, Attorney at Law · Last updated November 2026 · General information, not legal advice for your specific case.

How This Allegation Shows Up

  • DUI / DWI, especially a second offense or high BAC
  • Domestic violence or battery
  • Drug possession or paraphernalia
  • Theft, fraud, or insurance-related charges
  • Charges arising from patient care
  • Undisclosed past convictions on a license or renewal application

How Nevada Boards Handle It

Boards typically learn of criminal matters through self-reports, renewal-application questions, background checks, employers, or court records. They may ask for police reports, court documents, and a substance-use evaluation. The board's focus is whether you are safe to practice — not re-trying the criminal case.

What to Do Right Now

  1. Don't self-report until you know what your board actually requires
  2. Don't accept a plea before a license attorney reviews it
  3. Answer renewal questions truthfully — non-disclosure is its own violation
  4. Keep copies of every police and court document

DUI & Criminal Arrests FAQ

Do I have to report a DUI arrest to my Nevada licensing board?

It depends on your board and whether it is an arrest, charge, or conviction. Some reporting is triggered only by conviction or on renewal. Get advice for your specific license before reporting.

Can a DUI cost me my nursing or medical license in Nevada?

A single DUI rarely leads to revocation on its own, but it can lead to an investigation, evaluation, or monitoring, especially if there are signs of substance-use disorder or it is not disclosed.

What if I didn't disclose an old conviction on my application?

Non-disclosure can be treated as dishonesty, which is often more serious than the original offense. Talk to a lawyer about correcting it the right way.
