Quick answer

Privacy complaints — looking up records without a work reason, sharing patient details with friends or on social media, or mishandling records — can lead to termination and a Nevada licensing board complaint for unprofessional conduct, even though HIPAA itself is enforced by federal regulators. Electronic health records keep detailed access logs, so these cases often turn on why a record was opened. Context, intent, and how you respond make a large difference in the outcome.

By Craig K. Perry, Attorney at Law · Last updated November 2026 · General information, not legal advice for your specific case.

How This Allegation Shows Up

  • Accessing records of coworkers, family, celebrities, or yourself
  • Posting about patients on social media, even without names
  • Discussing patients outside of work
  • Texting or emailing patient information on personal devices
  • Lost or improperly disposed records

How Nevada Boards Handle It

Employers usually discover privacy issues through EHR access audits, then terminate or report. The board reviews the audit, your explanation, and the employer's policies, and decides whether the conduct is unprofessional conduct warranting discipline.

See the full Nevada board discipline process, step by step.

How We Defend These Cases

  • Obtain the full access audit and identify legitimate reasons for access
  • Show training gaps or unclear policies
  • Demonstrate remorse, retraining, and low risk of repetition
  • Keep the matter non-public when possible

What to Do Right Now

  1. Don't delete posts or messages without legal advice
  2. Don't write explanations for HR until you've talked to a lawyer
  3. Collect your HIPAA training records

HIPAA & Privacy Breaches FAQ

Can a HIPAA violation cost me my Nevada license?

It can lead to discipline for unprofessional conduct, though most privacy cases result in lesser outcomes such as reprimands or education, especially for first-time, non-malicious conduct.

I looked up my own chart. Is that a violation?

Many employers prohibit it unless done through the patient portal. It's usually treated less seriously than accessing others' records, but it can still lead to discipline.

Does the Nevada board enforce HIPAA?

No — federal regulators enforce HIPAA. But the board can discipline the same conduct as unprofessional conduct under your licensing statute.
