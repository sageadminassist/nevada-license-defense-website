Quick answer

A wrong answer on a Nevada license or renewal application — most often about criminal convictions or discipline in another state — can be treated as a fraudulent application, leading to refusal to renew, denial of a new license, or discipline. Many professionals are confused by the conviction question: a criminal case that was closed or dismissed may still count as a conviction. Boards also learn of old arrests through fingerprints and ask why they were not explained. A mistake on an application does not have to end a career, but each case is fact-specific.

By Craig K. Perry, Attorney at Law · Last updated November 2026 · General information, not legal advice for your specific case.

How This Allegation Shows Up

  • Answering 'no' to a conviction question after a closed or dismissed case
  • Not reporting discipline from another state
  • Leaving out an arrest the board later finds through fingerprints
  • Inaccurate health or substance-use answers
  • Errors on renewal applications

How Nevada Boards Handle It

When the board finds an inaccurate answer, it asks for an explanation and records. It weighs whether the mistake was an honest misunderstanding or an intent to deceive. Outcomes range from closure to public reprimand, refusal to renew, or denial.

How We Defend These Cases

  • Determine whether the answer was actually wrong under the board's question
  • Show an honest misunderstanding with supporting documents
  • Correct the record in the right way, at the right time
  • Address the underlying conviction or discipline on its merits

What to Do Right Now

  1. Get certified court records for every arrest or case
  2. Don't amend an application on your own without advice
  3. Talk to a license defense attorney before responding to the board

Fraudulent Applications FAQ

My case was dismissed. Do I have to report it as a conviction?

Not always — but often a resolved case still involved a conviction or must be disclosed depending on the exact question. Read the question carefully and get advice before answering.

The board found an old arrest through my fingerprints. What now?

Expect questions about why the arrest happened and why it wasn't disclosed. A careful, documented explanation is essential.

Can a fraudulent application allegation lead to denial?

Yes. It can result in denial of a new license, refusal to renew, or discipline. Many cases resolve far better with early representation.
