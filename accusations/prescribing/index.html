Quick answer

Nevada prescribers — physicians, DOs, PAs, APRNs, dentists, and others — can face board investigation for opioid and controlled-substance prescribing based on Prescription Monitoring Program (PMP) data, pharmacy reports, patient overdoses, or DEA activity. Boards look at whether you followed Nevada's prescribing laws and regulations, checked the PMP, documented medical need, and used appropriate patient agreements. Expert review of your charts usually decides these cases, so the records and your response must be handled carefully from the start.

By Craig K. Perry, Attorney at Law · Last updated November 2026 · General information, not legal advice for your specific case.

How This Allegation Shows Up

  • High-dose or long-term opioid prescribing flagged by PMP data
  • Failure to check the PMP or document required assessments
  • Prescribing to yourself, family, or staff
  • Patient overdose or death
  • Pharmacy refusal reports or Board of Pharmacy referrals
  • Telehealth or out-of-scope prescribing

How Nevada Boards Handle It

Your own licensing board usually leads, but the Nevada State Board of Pharmacy and the DEA may run parallel inquiries. Boards often subpoena a sample of charts and send them to an outside expert reviewer. The expert's opinion frequently determines whether the case is dismissed or charged.

How We Defend These Cases

  • Review the charts and PMP history before anything is produced
  • Retain independent experts to rebut the board's reviewer
  • Document the clinical reasoning behind each prescribing decision
  • Address parallel Board of Pharmacy and DEA issues together
  • Negotiate education or practice-monitoring terms instead of restriction

What to Do Right Now

  1. Don't alter, add to, or 'clean up' any chart
  2. Don't contact patients named in the complaint
  3. Check whether your malpractice policy covers board defense
  4. Call a license defense attorney before producing records

Prescribing & Controlled Substances FAQ

Can the Nevada board investigate me based only on PMP data?

Yes. PMP data can prompt an investigation on its own, but data alone doesn't prove improper prescribing — documentation and expert review do.

Does the Board of Pharmacy have authority over physicians?

It can regulate practitioners who dispense or hold controlled-substance registrations in Nevada, in addition to the practitioner's own board.

What happens after an expert reviews my charts?

If the expert finds no violation, cases often close. If the expert criticizes care, you can respond with your own expert and argument before the board decides whether to file charges.
