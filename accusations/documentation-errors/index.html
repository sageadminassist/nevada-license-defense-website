Quick answer

Documentation complaints range from honest charting mistakes to allegations of falsifying records, late entries, copied vital signs, or billing for care not provided. Nevada boards treat intentional falsification as a dishonesty violation, which can carry heavier discipline than the underlying care issue. The difference between an error and falsification usually comes down to audit trails and context, so preserving and analyzing the electronic record early is critical.

By Craig K. Perry, Attorney at Law · Last updated November 2026 · General information, not legal advice for your specific case.

How This Allegation Shows Up

  • Late or back-dated entries
  • Copy-forward or reused vital signs and assessments
  • Medication administration records that don't match dispensing data
  • Home-health or telehealth visits documented but disputed
  • Altered or missing records after an adverse event
  • Timecard or continuing-education certificate discrepancies

How Nevada Boards Handle It

Boards obtain EHR audit trails, policies, and witness statements, then decide whether the issue is negligence, a policy violation, or intentional dishonesty. The characterization matters: it drives both the sanction and how the discipline reads publicly.

See the full Nevada board discipline process, step by step.

How We Defend These Cases

  • Obtain the full EHR audit trail — not just the printed chart
  • Show system, staffing, or policy factors behind the entries
  • Distinguish honest error from intent to deceive
  • Present remediation and training already completed

What to Do Right Now

  1. Never alter or add to a record after learning of a complaint
  2. Don't explain the entries to your employer in writing without advice
  3. Preserve your schedules, assignments, and communications

Charting & Documentation FAQ

Is a charting mistake grounds for discipline in Nevada?

It can be, but boards distinguish isolated errors from patterns and from intentional falsification. Many honest errors resolve without public discipline.

Can I add a late entry to fix a chart?

Only following your facility's late-entry policy, clearly labeled and dated, and never after you learn of a complaint without legal advice.

Why does 'dishonesty' matter so much?

Boards view dishonesty as a character issue that goes to fitness to practice, so it can lead to harsher discipline than the clinical issue itself.
