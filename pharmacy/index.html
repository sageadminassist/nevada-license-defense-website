The Nevada State Board of Pharmacy (BOP) has a duty to carry out and enforce the provisions of Nevada law to protect the health, safety and welfare of the public. The BOP directly affects doctors, PAs, and APRNs who administer medications in Nevada.

Cases We Handle Before the Board of Pharmacy

With increasing frequency, attorney Craig Perry represents clients alleged to have violated laws enforced by the BOP. Recent examples include:

Failure to Pull PMP Report

Creating a PMP (Prescription Monitoring Program) report and checking a patient's drug history before prescribing is an essential duty. Failure to pull a PMP report before prescribing or dispensing medications for each patient is one of the most common BOP violations.

Failure to Apply for Prescribing

Unlike other states, Nevada has strict requirements to apply for certification before prescribing. Failure of APRNs to obtain Nevada-specific licenses to prescribe medications is a Nevada requirement that differs from many other states.

Failure to Apply for Dispensing

Unlike other states, Nevada has strict requirements to apply for certification before dispensing. This catches many out-of-state practitioners who are unaware of Nevada's unique rules.

Improperly Signing Off on Billing

Signing off on billing under the name of another provider who did not see the patient — or logging in under another person's name to produce billing — creates serious legal problems.

Using Unlawful Dangerous Drugs

Med spas that buy or use expired or unlawful dangerous drugs not approved for sale in the United States face BOP action. You cannot purchase dangerous drugs from unauthorized sources, even if they appear available.

Medical Director Supervision Failures

Many medical professionals are recruited into medical director positions without fully understanding the supervision responsibilities required. Medical directors who fail to properly oversee their clinic face BOP and NSBME action.

Nevada Has Unique Pharmacy Requirements

Nevada differs from many other states in its pharmacy regulations — particularly around APRN prescribing and dispensing licenses. These differences catch many out-of-state practitioners off guard.
